# Frame: rubric

The frame's slots draft the plan's Intent, the prose a new teammate reads first, and its no-gos
draft Constraints. Everything here holds only what the person said or confirmed. A slot they left
open stays open: it becomes a Question with its lean, never a sentence that guesses.

## A good Intent

- It says what the work is and who it's for in its first two sentences, in the person's own
  words where they had good ones.
- It names the problem as it stands today before it says what the work does about it.
- It says what good looks like as something someone could see happen.
- It says what the work isn't: the nearest thing someone might mistake it for.
- It gives the appetite, when the person gave one, as a fact about the work, such as "a few
  weekends of work", not as a deadline.
- It's short: three short paragraphs at most, so the whole plan stays inside keyframes' 2000
  words.

## A good no-go

Each no-go is a Constraint, and an agent stops and asks before crossing one.

- It's one line that someone could tell they'd crossed: "Never writes over a file it didn't
  create", not "Respects the user's files".
- It says never or always, and means it. A line someone may cross once the person says so is
  written as what must happen first: "Asks before it writes over a file it didn't create."
- It carries a `because:` when the person said why.
- It stays under keyframes' 60 words for an item.

## Defaults to steer away from

- Adjectives with nothing behind them: "a powerful, flexible, easy-to-use tool".
- "For developers" or "for everyone", when the person named someone narrower.
- A problem written as the lack of the solution: "There's no tool that does X."
- Done as a feeling: "people love it", "it works well".
- Words the person never used and wouldn't: "seamless", "robust", "leverage", "best-in-class".
- No-gos copied from a checklist, such as secure, accessible and fast, when the person didn't
  raise them.
- A plausible guess written into a slot the person left open.
